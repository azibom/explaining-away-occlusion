The visual problem

The retinal image contains no label saying “in front.”

At an overlap, the visual system must infer two causes: what continued behind the border, and what interrupted it.

03

Computational experiment

23 architectures. One controlled occlusion task.

Kang and colleagues combined two FashionMNIST objects, randomly transformed them, and retained scenes with 50–75% occlusion. Networks had to classify both foreground and background.

05

The paper’s central mechanism

Recognize the front.
Subtract its explanation.

Once the foreground object explains some pixels and contours, the remaining evidence becomes less ambiguous. The background hypothesis improves.

06

Not just a correlation

Change the order—or corrupt the memory.

Normal-order models predicted foreground first and background second. Reversing that order removed the advantage. Replacing the recurrent hidden state with one from a different occluder selectively hurt background recognition.

image→background→foregroundweaker

08

Analysis by synthesis

A model can iteratively reconstruct what the occluder removed.

Recon-Net receives the occluded image plus the foreground object. Across five inference steps, it estimates a 62-dimensional latent code whose generator approaches the unoccluded background.

10

The thread to computational neuroscience

Perception is inference
with causes competing.

The brain may solve missing visual input not by filling pixels blindly, but by identifying one cause, explaining it away, and revising another. That is a concrete bridge between computation, recurrent circuits, and the contents of experience.

About this project

An independent educational reimplementation.

This browser experience translates the human psychophysics logic from Kang et al. (2026) into a short classroom demonstration. It is not the authors’ official task, a diagnostic test, or a scientific replication.

The paper’s full experiment allowed up to 400 trials and used quality controls. This demo uses original procedural stimuli, 20 measured trials, and descriptive personal feedback. Timing varies across browsers and displays.

No analytics, accounts, server, cookies, or response uploads are used.
